# Sensory efficacy and afferent rates: literature check (stage 2B follow-up)

Purpose: bound (a) afferent firing range and (b) afferent-to-target efficacy relative to central synapses for the Pugliese-style rate model (0.03 x synapse count; threshold about 7.5 x size).

Verification key: VERIFIED = I opened the text (full text or abstract/records via Europe PMC / bioRxiv / lab-site PDF) and the claim is in it. ABSTRACT-ONLY = only abstract/metadata seen. NOT VERIFIED = could not open the source (paywall, bot-check, HTTP errors) or no source found. Quotes are at most 15 words. Several PMC/Nature/eLife pages returned bot-check or errors; I used Europe PMC XML and bioRxiv instead.

## 1. Firing rates / response ranges of leg proprioceptors

Key point: I found NO published spike-rate (Hz) recordings from identified Drosophila leg proprioceptor axons. Everything below is calcium imaging.

- Mamiya, Gurung, Tuthill 2018, Neuron 100(3):636-650. DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2018.09.009. FeCO claw/hook/club; method: in vivo two-photon GCaMP6f calcium imaging, NOT spikes. VERIFIED (PMC6481666, first ~100k characters read). Text gives no absolute dF/F range. Stimuli: tibia ramps/swings to 360 deg/s, velocities 100-800 deg/s, vibration 100-2000 Hz. Imaging rate 8.01 Hz (so cannot resolve spikes). Hook direction-selectivity index 0.811; club 0.117.
- Mamiya et al. 2023, Neuron 111(20):3230-3243, "Biomechanical origins of proprioceptor feature selectivity and topographic maps in the Drosophila leg". DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2023.07.009. Exists; ABSTRACT-ONLY (via search summaries, PMC10644877 not opened). Calcium imaging. No rate numbers obtained.
- Hair plates: Pratt, Dallmann, Chou, Siwanowicz, Walling-Bell, Cook, Sustar, Azevedo, Tuthill. "Proprioceptive limit detectors contribute to sensorimotor control of the Drosophila leg", Nature Communications 17, 2026. DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-69333-z (preprint: "...mediate sensorimotor control...", bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.05.15.654260). Bibliographic data VERIFIED (PDF metadata, Europe PMC). Content read from the bioRxiv preprint (VERIFIED): CxHP8 imaging is calcium only, normalized to dataset maximum (0-1), so no absolute magnitudes; "we found no evidence for phasic tuning among CxHP8 axons" (sustained at the joint limit); activity largest in front-leg grooming. No electrophysiology. (My earlier suspicion that the 2024 item was a paper: the 2024 item is Pratt's UW PhD thesis; the paper is 2025 preprint / 2026 journal.) Note: this paper treats CxHP8 specifically; SNpp45/SNpp52 type mapping to CxHP8 or other plates is NOT verified.
- Campaniform sensilla: no Drosophila leg CS physiology (spikes or calcium) found. Dinges et al. 2020 (CS distribution, Drosophila) and Dinges et al. 2024, Bioinspir. Biomim., doi 10.1088/1748-3190/ad1db9 (nano-CT / finite-element model of femoral CS fields) are anatomy/mechanics, not firing rates. ABSTRACT-ONLY (search summaries). Blow-fly tibial CS extracellular recordings exist (other species; details NOT VERIFIED).
- Dallmann et al. 2025 (see section 3) confirms claw (position) axons stay active across behaviours; calcium only; paper states proprioceptor axons are too small for intracellular recording. VERIFIED (preprint v2 text).
- Locust (other species): Wolf & Burrows 1995 J Neurosci 15:5623, intracellular recordings from FCO afferents in walking locusts, spikes on a depolarizing input. ABSTRACT-ONLY; the abstract has no Hz values.

## 2. Afferent to motor neuron / premotor strength

- Drosophila, FeCO to leg MNs, physiology: Azevedo et al. 2020, eLife 9:e56754, "A size principle for recruitment of Drosophila leg motor neurons" (this is the 2020 "size principle" paper; it is eLife, not Nature). Europe PMC XML PMC7347388, first ~100k characters read. VERIFIED. Whole-cell recordings during FeCO (probe) movement: slow MN spikes follow tibia angle ("1° change in tibia angle resulted in a significant change"); example intermediate MN EPSP "Extension causes an 8 mV EPSP" (single neuron, resting about -67 mV, 60 um probe = 8 deg); fast and intermediate MNs: "sensory-evoked PSPs typically failed to elicit spikes"; "we never observed feedback-evoked spikes in fast motor neurons"; PSP size largest in slow MNs, smallest in fast. Caveat: PSPs are from whole-organ movement (about 150 afferents), not per afferent spike; the 8 mV is one example neuron; population mean and Hz not in the part read (later 245k characters unread).
- Drosophila, FeCO to central neurons: Agrawal et al. 2020, eLife 9:e60299, doi 10.7554/eLife.60299. VERIFIED via Europe PMC XML (partial). Authors state they "lack direct evidence that FeCO sensory neurons provide monosynaptic input" to 13Ba/9Aa/10Ba (EM-based inference). Optogenetics (of the central neurons, not FeCO): 13Ba activation gives slow femur-tibia flexion plus coxa extension; 9Aa activation gives small, variable tibia-tarsus/femur-tibia extensions; 10Ba activation does not reliably move the leg (pauses walking). No FeCO-driven PSP amplitudes in mV. Optogenetic FeCO activation itself is not described.
- Hair plates, connectome: Pratt et al. preprint (VERIFIED, first 100k chars): about 75% of CxHP8 output synapses go to premotor (58%) and motor (17%) neurons, about 2,279 +/- 482 synapses per axon; "provide strong monosynaptic input to motor neurons within the coxa posterior module"; strongest inhibition goes to the promotor module. Optogenetic activation in standing flies moved the leg posteriorly and laterally. So in Drosophila hair-plate to MN contacts are anatomically large, but the physiological per-spike efficacy is not measured. "Strong" is a connectome (synapse count) statement here, not a PSP measurement.
- Locust (other species, labelled): Burrows 1987 J Neurosci 7:1064 (DOI 10.1523/jneurosci.07-04-01064.1987): FCO afferent spikes evoke EPSPs in flexor MNs at about 1.5 ms constant latency; afferents contact MNs and spiking local interneurons in parallel, each target receives several afferents. ABSTRACT-ONLY; no per-spike mV in abstract. Burrows & Matheson 1994 J Neurosci 14:272 (DOI 10.1523/jneurosci.14-01-00272.1994): presynaptic inputs reduce afferent spike amplitude 12-28% and monosynaptic EPSP amplitude in MNs by up to 50%. ABSTRACT-ONLY. Unitary EPSP amplitudes in mV: NOT VERIFIED (full texts blocked).
- Stick insect: fCO feeds nonspiking premotor interneurons that act on MNs; no monosynaptic FeCO to MN EPSP amplitude found (source: a 2021 Cologne dissertation, excerpt only; NOT VERIFIED beyond that).

Interpretation (mine, not a source claim): direct sensory-to-MN drive is anatomically present but the physiological result in fly (Azevedo) is graded subthreshold-to-sometimes-suprathreshold PSPs of the order of mV for whole-organ movement, with fast MNs never driven to spike by feedback. A model in which proprioceptors at plausible rates exceed MN threshold several-fold contradicts this.

## 3. Presynaptic inhibition / gain control

- Dallmann et al. 2025, Nature 647(8089):445-453, "Selective presynaptic inhibition of leg proprioception in behaving Drosophila". DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09554-2 (preprint 10.1101/2023.10.20.563322, title "Presynaptic inhibition selectively suppresses leg proprioception in behaving Drosophila"). VERIFIED (preprint v2 text, journal record).
  - Calcium imaging (GCaMP6f/7f) only; no voltage/spikes.
  - Hook (movement) axons are suppressed during walking and grooming; claw (position) and club axons are not (claw r = 0.91 passive-model fit).
  - FANC: hook axon input is 94.9% interneurons, 4.6% sensory neurons; 83.1% of GABAergic input synapses on hook axons come from the 9A hemilineage; the chief 9A neuron gives 56.8% of hook axons' presynaptic input. MANC: chief-9A-like neurons send about 58% of output to hook axons.
  - The text does not call them axo-axonic explicitly (it says "presynaptic inputs onto the axons"). No quantitative % suppression given in the portion read. Effect on postsynaptic efficacy not measured.
  - Hair plate axons appear only as a connectome group, not recorded; campaniform sensilla not addressed.
- Pratt et al. (hair plates) report "did not observe suppression of hair plate calcium activity during spontaneous leg movements" and that some partners "synapse back onto CxHP8 axons" (recurrent premotor feedback). VERIFIED (preprint). So the PI evidence is specific to FeCO hook axons, not hair plates.
- Locust (other species): Burrows & Matheson 1994 (gain control, section 2) and Wolf & Burrows 1995 (rhythmic presynaptic inhibition during walking; reduces spike effectiveness). ABSTRACT-ONLY. Also J Neurocytol 1993 GABA-immunoreactivity presynaptic to FCO afferent terminals (DOI 10.1007/bf01189042; listed, not opened).
- Lesser et al. 2024 Nature 631 (DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07600-z, FANC premotor networks); Cheong et al. 2024 and Marin et al. 2024 (MANC; Marin preprint DOI 10.1101/2023.06.05.543407): bibliographic records confirmed via Europe PMC search, but I could NOT open their text for axo-axonic claims. NOT VERIFIED for any statement about axo-axonic synapses.

## 4. Connectome simulations with sensory-specific synaptic scale

- Shiu et al. 2024 Nature, "A Drosophila computational brain model reveals sensorimotor processing", DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9 (PMC11446845). VERIFIED (text read to ~100k chars): single global weight W_syn = 0.275 mV per synapse, chosen by calibrating so that sugar GRN activation at 100 Hz gives about 80% of maximal MN9 firing; "we treat each neuron identically as a spiking neuron"; sensory neurons driven by Poisson input to give 10-260 Hz firing (GRNs) and 20-220 Hz (JO neurons for grooming). So: no separate sensory scale; the sensory input rate was instead the free knob, and the global scale was fit to a behavioural readout.
- Pugliese et al., "Connectome simulations identify a central pattern generator for fly walking", Nature 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-11195-y; preprint 10.1101/2025.09.12.675944). VERIFIED (preprint, partial): model includes no sensory afferents; threshold N(7.5, 0.6) x median-normalized size; r_max about 200 Hz; a constant scales synaptic input relative to external input and is the same for all neurons (its value 0.03 not visible in the text I got); Discussion says proprioceptive feedback needs "biologically realistic interfaces of proprioceptive sensors". So this model gives no guidance on afferent scaling.
- Search found no connectome-simulation paper assigning sensory neurons a different synaptic scale. This is an absence of evidence from a limited search, not proof.

## 5. Quantitative constraints usable in the model

(a) Afferent firing range
- No Drosophila leg afferent spike rates (Hz) exist in what I verified. All data are calcium (8 Hz imaging in Mamiya 2018). Any Hz value used for FeCO/hair plate/CS must be labelled an assumption.
- Hair plate: sustained, non-phasic code at joint limit (Pratt 2025/26, normalized 0-1; no Hz). Claw: position, tonic. Hook: movement/velocity (tested 100-800 deg/s, Mamiya 2018) but suppressed to some unquantified degree during walking/grooming (Dallmann 2025), which supports using a lower effective hook gain in walking simulations.
- Other-species Hz (locust/cockroach) NOT VERIFIED. If needed, use r_max = 200 Hz (Pugliese model ceiling) as the hard upper bound; this is a model constant, not a measurement.
- Shiu et al. precedent for sensory drive: 10-260 Hz (taste), 20-220 Hz (JO), Drosophila, brain, imposed by Poisson input (model choice, not recordings of leg afferents).

(b) Afferent-to-target efficacy relative to central synapses
- Azevedo 2020 (Drosophila, whole-cell): whole-FeCO movement (8 deg tibia) gives about 8 mV EPSP in one intermediate MN; fast MNs not driven to spike by feedback. Slow > intermediate > fast PSP size. Use as a target: afferent drive from realistic stimulation should produce a depolarization of the order of 8 mV (below spike threshold of fast MNs, near or above for slow MNs), not a multiple of threshold.
- Burrows & Matheson 1994 (locust): presynaptic inhibition scales monosynaptic afferent-to-MN EPSP by up to 0.5 (50% reduction) and spike amplitude by 12-28%. Usable as a plausible gain factor range of 0.5-1.0 for context-dependent suppression (other species, FeCO).
- Anatomy: CxHP8 gives about 2,279 synapses per axon, 17% onto MNs (about 390) and 58% onto premotor, i.e. synapse counts are large; per-synapse efficacy for sensory contacts is therefore plausibly lower than 0.03 per synapse if rates are high. This is an inference, not a published sensory scale.
- Shiu et al. W_syn = 0.275 mV with uniform scale for all neurons; the published fitting target was a behavioural readout at 100 Hz sensory drive, which suggests one defensible procedure: calibrate a sensory-specific scale s_sens so that physiologically bounded drive (e.g., the Azevedo 8 mV, or rates at the lower end of any assumed Hz range) gives a sub-threshold to threshold-crossing MN depolarization. No published source gives a sensory-specific scale.
